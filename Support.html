Quick start

Six steps, one take

Quick Start Guide~5 min
01

Install the Blender addon

Download the free addon zip from GitHub Releases. In Blender, go to Edit → Preferences → Add-ons → Install and select the zip file.

02

Enable the addon

Search for “BlendMotion” in the addon list and check the box to enable it.

03

Check the Preflight Checklist

Open the BlendMotion panel in the 3D viewport sidebar (N key). The Preflight Checklist automatically verifies your IP address, active camera, port availability, and scene FPS.

04

Install the iPhone app

Download BlendMotion from the App Store.

05

Connect

Copy your IP address from the checklist (click the copy button), enter it in the iPhone app, and tap Connect.

06

Start & record

Click Start in the Blender panel. Move your iPhone to control the camera. Tap Record to capture keyframes on the timeline.

Tips

Good to know

No IP hunting. The Preflight Checklist shows your local IP automatically with a one-click copy button. Just paste it into the iPhone app.
Hold it like a camera. BlendMotion works in both landscape and portrait — pick your orientation in the app’s Settings. Landscape gives the classic cinematic framing.
Same Wi-Fi network. BlendMotion communicates over your local network via UDP. If your iPhone and computer are on different networks, they won’t find each other.
Troubleshooting

Getting unstuck

Can’t connect?

Check the Preflight Checklist first — it flags most issues automatically. Ensure both devices are on the same Wi-Fi network and your firewall isn’t blocking the UDP port.

On iOS, also verify that BlendMotion has Local Network permission (Settings → Privacy & Security → Local Network), and disable any VPN that might route local traffic through a tunnel.

Laggy tracking?

Close other apps on your iPhone and reduce the distance to your Wi-Fi router for a stronger signal. Use a 5 GHz network if available.

Addon not showing up?

Make sure you’re running Blender 4.0 or later. Try restarting Blender after installation.

No active camera?

The Preflight Checklist will warn you if your scene has no camera. Add one with Shift+A → Camera and set it as active.
